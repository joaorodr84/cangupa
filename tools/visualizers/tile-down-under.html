Cangupa — Down Under tile designs

Pillar, Mud and Tunnel

Three new tiles, several candidate looks for each. Pick one per tile; the board at the bottom redraws with your picks next to the five tiles the game already has, on the dark and the light pit and at three sizes. The smallest is about what a turned 8×7 board gives on a small phone, which is where a design that looked fine tends to fall apart.

Tile 1 of 3 — #

Pillar

A solid block that is not a tile: never landed on, never needs a visit, and a jump cannot pass over it. So it must not look like ground. It sits in the pit, smaller than a tile, with no lip and no mark, and reads as taller than its neighbours from a lighter top and a shadow thrown to one side.

Tile 2 of 3 — m

Mud

One visit. You can step off it but not jump off it. Every candidate uses the same brown and the same sunk face (half a lip, set lower than the tiles beside it); what differs is the mark, which has to carry the rule without relying on colour.

Tile 3 of 3 — a/a, b/b

Tunnel

A pair of mouths: land on one and you come out of its twin, and both are used up. Every candidate is a dark hole in a violet tile. What needs choosing is how two pairs on one board are told apart without colour. Each card shows a mouth of pair a beside a mouth of pair b.

Together

On a board

One 5×5 board holding every tile kind: two pillars, two mud tiles, two tunnel pairs, and the existing Common, Gold, Steel, Departure and Arrival. Switch on greyscale to check that nothing depends on hue alone.
